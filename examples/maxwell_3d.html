Maxwell equations in 3D — patched Nédélec-II / BDM1 example

Why a new Maxwell example?

The wave tutorials The wave equation — 2A: conservative anisotropic membrane and The wave equation — 2C: co-energy and localized damping work in two spatial dimensions. This is an intentionally tiny genuine 3D test: a homogeneous sphere, just two vector unknowns, no sources, no feedback controllers, and four weak-form bricks. It exercises the corrected intrinsic vector finite elements in the pinned SCRIMP GetFEM 5.5 image, not ordinary componentwise CG vector elements.

This example is new, independent of the published COMPEL_2025/Maxwell_2D.py research script (which is not modified), and of the 2D Nédélec-independent wave benchmark.

A normalized Maxwell system

Let \(\Omega\) be the unit ball in \(\mathbb R^3\), filled with a homogeneous, isotropic, lossless material with dimensionless \(\varepsilon=\mu=1\). The electric field \(E\) and the magnetic induction \(B\) satisfy

\[\partial_t E = \operatorname{curl} B,\qquad \partial_t B = -\operatorname{curl} E,\]

without free currents or sources. Initially,

\[E(0,x,y,z)=(-y,x,0),\qquad B(0)=0.\]

The electric field has nonzero curl and zero divergence initially. It is an exact low-order edge-element-type polynomial and excites a nontrivial magnetic field.

The true 3D domain and both co-energy/state declarations come directly from the executable:

model.set_domain(S.Domain("Ball", {"R": 1., "h": float(mesh_size)}))
if model.domain.get_dim()[0] != 3:
    raise RuntimeError("Maxwell requires a genuine 3D volume mesh")


E = S.State("E", "Electric field", "vector-field")
B = S.State("B", "Magnetic induction", "vector-field")
model.add_state(E)
model.add_state(B)
model.add_costate(S.CoState("e_E", "Electric field", E, substituted=True))
model.add_costate(S.CoState("e_B", "Magnetic induction", B, substituted=True))

The coupling uses the homogeneous natural boundary closure of the weak form, namely zero magnetic tangential boundary work, not an explicitly imposed electric PEC trace and not a driven port. The discrete model closes the boundary power balance by construction. This small demonstration does not claim strong boundary-trace or electromagnetic eigenmode accuracy.

Compatible 3D spaces and their GetFEM patches

We use the SCRIMP-patched pair on genuine tetrahedra:

\[E_h \in \mathrm{N\acute ed\acute elec}_{\mathrm{II},1} \subset H(\mathrm{curl};\Omega),\qquad B_h \in \mathrm{BDM}_1 \subset H(\mathrm{div};\Omega).\]

On each tetrahedron, both fields have local polynomial shape functions in \([P_1]^3\); the Nédélec-II edge DOFs impose tangential continuity, while BDM1 face DOFs impose normal continuity. In particular \(\operatorname{curl} E_h\) is piecewise constant and belongs to the divergence-free subspace of BDM1, compatible with Maxwell’s magnetic Gauss law.

Important distinction: the original accepted baseline at source commit daac18cb used GetFEM’s built-in lowest Nédélec FEM_NEDELEC(3) with built-in RT0. It did not exercise SCRIMP’s new element patches. The current tutorial now explicitly uses FEM_NEDELEC2(3,1) (patch 0004) and tetrahedral FEM_BDMK(3,1) (patch 0001).

These FE names use the new SCRIMP shortcuts S.FEM("E", 1, "NED2") and S.FEM("B", 1, "BDM"). Additional shortcuts NED1 / NEDELEC1 and NED2 / NEDELEC2 are mapped by scrimp/fem.py to the corresponding GetFEM constructors. The number in NED1 or NED2 is the family (1st/2nd kind), while the numeric argument is the polynomial order k. Patch 0004 supports only k=1 or 2.

# Nedelec0/RT0. NED2 k=1 -> FEM_NEDELEC2(3,1) (patch 0004),
# BDM k=1 -> FEM_BDMK(3,1) (tetrahedral DoF patch 0001).
# curl([P1]^3) is constant and is contained in BDM1;
# exact div(curl(E)) = 0 is preserved by the mass equation.
fem_E = S.FEM("E", 1, "NED2")
fem_B = S.FEM("B", 1, "BDM")

# S.FEM has a legacy silent CG fallback on native AssertionError.
# Retain these exact objects to detect it after registration.
for fem, expected_type, expected_order in (
    (fem_E, "NED2", 1),
    (fem_B, "BDM", 1),
):
    model.add_FEM(fem)
    assert_patched_intrinsic_fem(fem, expected_type, expected_order)

A strict post-registration guard retains both original S.FEM objects and verifies that their type and order are unchanged after DPHS.add_FEM(), and that the actual GetFEM MeshFem has vector dimension 3 and nonzero degrees of freedom. This is important: SCRIMP’s existing FEM adapter mutates the original type/order to CG/1 on GetFEM’s AssertionError fallback. A silent replacement is thus explicitly detected, without relying on the Python binding’s version-dependent MeshFem.fem() layout. This runtime guard is not yet a complete proof of Maxwell numerical correctness.

A skew-adjoint discrete Maxwell interconnection

The weak formulation is deliberately minimal:

\[\begin{split}(\partial_t E_h,v_h)_{L^2(\Omega)} &= (B_h,\operatorname{curl}v_h)_{L^2(\Omega)},\\ (\partial_t B_h,w_h)_{L^2(\Omega)} &= -(\operatorname{curl}E_h,w_h)_{L^2(\Omega)}.\end{split}\]

The GetFEM generic weak-form language does not supply a portable built-in 3D Curl in this image. The canonical example defines the operator explicitly from the 3x3 gradient tensor, without imposing an ad hoc stencil or swapping the curl orientation:

CURL3 = (
    "[Grad(v)(3,2)-Grad(v)(2,3);"
    "Grad(v)(1,3)-Grad(v)(3,1);"
    "Grad(v)(2,1)-Grad(v)(1,2)]"
)

There are only four variational bricks: two mass blocks and the two mutually opposite curl couplings. They are skew-adjoint in the discrete GetFEM mass inner products.

for brick in (
    S.Brick("M_E", "E.Test_E", [1], dt=True, position="flow"),
    S.Brick("M_B", "B.Test_B", [1], dt=True, position="flow"),
    S.Brick("C^T", "B.Curl3(Test_E)", [1], position="effort"),
    S.Brick("-C", "-Curl3(E).Test_B", [1], position="effort"),
):
    model.add_brick(brick)

The original electromagnetic Hamiltonian is

\[\mathcal H(t) = \frac12\int_{\Omega} (|E_h|^2+|B_h|^2)\,\mathrm dx.\]

The semidiscrete identity follows by testing the first equation with E_h and the second with B_h:

\[\frac{\mathrm d\mathcal H}{\mathrm dt} =(B_h,\operatorname{curl}E_h) -(\operatorname{curl}E_h,B_h)=0.\]

The initial data and CN integration are explicitly defined in the runnable source, including the absence of any unnecessary algebraic DAE projection:

# zero magnetic field; curl E=[0,0,2] triggers nontrivial dynamics.
model.set_initial_value("E", "[-y, x, 0.]")
model.set_initial_value("B", "[0., 0., 0.]")

model.set_time_scheme(
    # Both states carry a positive mass: this is an ODE, NOT a DAE.
    # The algebraic initializer requires algebraic unknowns;
    # a pseudo-TS or artificial DAE spin-up would be wrong here.
    init_step=False,
    ts_type="cn", ts_adapt_type="none",
    ts_exact_final_time="matchstep",
    t_0=0., t_f=float(final_time),
    dt=float(time_step), dt_save=float(time_step),
    ksp_type="gmres", pc_type="lu",
    pc_factor_mat_solver_type=factor_solver,
)
model.solve()

The original two positive electromagnetic energy terms are likewise the canonical GetFEM Hamiltonian, not recomputed from an invented displacement field:

model.hamiltonian.set_name("3D electromagnetic energy")
model.hamiltonian.add_term(S.Term("Electric", "0.5*E.E", [1]))
model.hamiltonian.add_term(S.Term("Magnetic", "0.5*B.B", [1]))
return model

The fixed Crank–Nicolson scheme has the corresponding exact quadratic discrete identity:

\[\mathcal H^{n+1}-\mathcal H^n =\Delta t\left[ (B_h^{n+1/2},\operatorname{curl}E_h^{n+1/2}) -(\operatorname{curl}E_h^{n+1/2},B_h^{n+1/2}) \right]=0.\]

Both variables are differential, so there is no algebraic DAE initial projection or pseudo-time spin-up. Initial data are projected into their exact native finite-element spaces.

How to run and qualify this 3D example

We separate a source contract from real native numerical acceptance. The qualification runner uses the same pinned GetFEM/PETSc/MUMPS Docker image as the 2D examples. It checks 10 fixed CN steps at h=0.7, dt=0.02, t_f=0.2, on MPI 1 and MPI 2:

cd ~/Simulations/scrimp
git pull --ff-only
conda activate scrimp

SCRIMP_DOCKER_NO_SUDO=1 \
SCRIMP_V130_DAE_IMAGE=scrimp-mpi:v1.3.0-rc3 \
bash scripts/run-v130-maxwell3d-pilot.sh

The runner will only print PASS_MAXWELL3D_MUMPS_MPI1_MPI2 if all native checks succeed. It saves even failed traces to sandbox/outputs/v130-maxwell3d-*.zip.

The physical gates include the presence of a genuine 3D mesh, real patched Nédélec-II/BDM1 spaces, exact ten-step fixed CN integration, positive initial electromagnetic energy, nontrivial magnetic excitation, and agreement between GetFEM Hamiltonian values and independent electric/magnetic volume quadratures. Every-step and cumulative energy defects must be below 1e-9 times the physical energy scale. All MPI rank traces and MPI1/MPI2 results are compared. The divergence of B is independently measured by GetFEM’s squared L2 norm and checked against the compatible discrete Gauss law. Its initial value is zero and, for the compatible Nédélec-II k1 and BDM1 spaces, the discrete curl maps into the divergence-free BDM subspace. This is an actual additional acceptance gate, not a claim of convergence of all Maxwell constraints. The electric Gauss law and electromagnetic boundary-mode accuracy are outside this minimal pilot.

After a native PASS, the archive contains true complete FE histories (rank*.npz), original energy/diagnostic values, JSON acceptance metadata, and two genuine 3D ParaView PVD time series for E and B (11 VTU snapshots each). Open mumps_mpi1/pv/E/E.pvd and mumps_mpi1/pv/B/B.pvd in ParaView; view the 3D vectors with a cut plane, glyphs, or streamlines.

The maximum coefficient difference between full MPI1 and MPI2 FE histories (11 times, 1,269 unknowns) is 2.1094237467877974e-15. Each MPI2 rank saved an identical trajectory, with its SHA256 verified. All five graphics are generated from the same run by examples/maxwell_3d.py --export, including the 11-frame native magnetic GIF. The integrated renderer is therefore qualified in MPI1 and MPI2, not just source-tested. The true vector VTU exports and original time samples have been independently inspected.

The earlier source commit daac18cb was a valid separate baseline using GetFEM native Nédélec0/RT0 (497 DOFs), not a test of either patched family. Neither the new or the old qualification establishes mesh/time convergence, electric Gauss-law validity, explicit PEC boundary conditions, cavity eigenmodes, or SuperLU_DIST acceptance.

Figures and animation generated by the example itself

The same Python executable now creates its own scientific figures and animation whenever --export --output /artifacts is requested. No separate notebook, rendering script or vtk Python package is needed: the existing matplotlib stack and the standard library read the original GetFEM 3D VTU/PVD. The energy curves are drawn from the independently assembled native GetFEM H_E, H_B and H arrays, not from resampled nodal magnitudes.

For a complete run:

python -m examples.maxwell_3d --check --export --output ./maxwell_results

The output folder contains:

  • pv/E/E.pvd and pv/B/B.pvd: original GetFEM vector time series, with one native VTU per accepted CN state.

  • visuals/maxwell3d_echange_energie.png and .svg: electric, magnetic and total Hamiltonian over the original FE time samples.

  • visuals/maxwell3d_vecteurs_E_tfinal.png and maxwell3d_vecteurs_B_tfinal.png: 3D final-time arrows at the authentic exported VTK nodal coordinates.

Electromagnetic energy transfer: from H_E(0)=0.6942520909491595, H_B(0)=0, we obtain H_E(0.2)=0.4359826378118022 and H_B(0.2)=0.2582694531373567, so 37.20% of the original energy moves from electric to magnetic storage, with no dissipative loss. The independent GetFEM volume quadratures of electric and magnetic energy add to H to within 5.55e-17 on MPI1; the full-step and cumulative total Hamiltonian remains constant at machine precision.

The magnetic Gauss law is checked by an independent real GetFEM volume integral of (div B)^2, with ||div B||_L2 <= 4.7e-15 over all saved times. This is a tested constraint for the discrete field, not a claim that the electric Gauss law was tested.

VTK verification beyond XML: all 44 VTU files (2 fields x 11 times x 2 MPI configurations) were opened with a genuine VTK unstructured-grid reader. Every file contains 58 3D points, 110 native tetrahedral cells (VTK type 10) and a finite vector field with three components. Mesh geometry matches across the eleven times and the two MPI configurations. The 4 PVD catalogs contain the corresponding 11 timestamps. At final time the nodal magnetic-vector norm ranges from about 0.367 to 0.375.

Limitations: these results qualify only the original native Nédélec0/RT0 3D Maxwell port-Hamiltonian baseline at one mesh size and one time step. It establishes neither mesh/time convergence, electric Gauss-law accuracy, explicit PEC boundary traces, cavity-mode correctness nor a SuperLU_DIST qualification. The boundary remains the documented natural zero-work closure, not an imposed PEC wall.
